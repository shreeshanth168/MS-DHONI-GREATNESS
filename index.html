From Ranchi to the world.

Dhoni worked as a ticket collector at Kharagpur railway station before reaching the India team in 2004. He arrived as a big-hitting keeper and went on to become India's most successful white-ball captain.

His method: stay calm, trust the process, take the chase deep.

Every chase needs a finisher.

His way of closing a game, in four steps.

  1. 01Read the game

    Study the pitch, the bowlers and the required rate before anything else.

  2. 02Stay calm

    No panic, no wasted shots. Let the pressure sit on the other side.

  3. 03Take it deep

    Keep the target within reach, ball after ball, until the last over.

  4. 04Finish it

    Pick the moment, clear the rope, and close the game out.

You need 20 off 12. He handles it.

An ODI average above 50 comes from finishing games.

  • Walk inNo hurry, no show of nerves.

The journey

The moments that built the legend.

2004

India debut

ODI debut against Bangladesh in December. Test debut follows in 2005, T20I debut in 2006.

2005

183 not out at Jaipur

A chase against Sri Lanka that is still the highest ODI score by a wicketkeeper.

2007

Captain and World T20 champion

He leads a young side to the first World T20 title, beating Pakistan in the final.

2011

World Cup glory at Wankhede

An unbeaten 91 in the final against Sri Lanka, finished with a six. India's first World Cup since 1983.

2013

Champions Trophy

India win in England, completing a set no other captain has: World T20, World Cup and Champions Trophy.

2014 to 2020

Stepping back

Retires from Tests in December 2014, leaves limited-overs captaincy in January 2017, and retires from international cricket on 15 August 2020.

Stories

Why he mattered, in a few minutes of reading.

Leadership

Why Captain Cool worked

He rarely raised his voice, and rarely needed to. His teams played without panic because he showed none.

He backed young players through poor runs, kept plans simple, and decided late, with the full picture in front of him.

Batting

The art of the finish

He went deep in chases, kept the rate in range, then used power only when needed.

An ODI average above 50, helped by many not outs, shows a batter who valued the win over his own score.

Keeping

Hands faster than the eye

With 829 international dismissals, he was also among the quickest stumpers the game has seen.

195 of those were stumpings, many from batters who only lifted a heel.

Some finishes stay forever.
